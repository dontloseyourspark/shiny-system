Design Process

I care about craft — which means sweating the details in Figma and asking the right questions before opening Figma.

My Iterative Design Process

My process isn’t a straight line. Each step feeds the next, but what I learn along the way, especially in testing, regularly sends me back: to refine a design, rethink an idea, or learn more about the people I’m designing for. It fits naturally with Agile teams, and every loop makes the product a little more effective and user-friendly.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    Before I do any design work, I need to understand the current position of the brand, its website and app, and who I’m designing for. I can’t jump in until I have a picture of where the company stands in relation to its competitors, its users, and their pain points and known issues.

    These are some of the tools I use to build that picture:

    User Interviews

    For a hypothetical note-taking app, I conducted user interviews to inform its design. I prepared an interview script with open-ended questions, focusing on my target audiences’ current note-taking process, pain points and frustrations with that process and features they would like in a new app. I recruited and interviewed some users remotely and some in person and then analysed my findings.

    I had hypothesised that users would want a better note-taking experience. Instead, every participant (100%) still preferred pen and paper to digital note-taking, so my conclusion was that they might not need a fancy note-taking app at all: their pen and paper is usually enough.

    Personas

    These personas were developed for a UK-based organisation that caters to accountants in the public sector, accounting firms, and professional bodies responsible for managing public funds, utilising custom questionnaires for statistical data collection. We wanted to form a deeper understanding of our users' goals, needs, experiences, and behaviours. So, we created 2 personas, one for each of our user segments.

    Personas help me design for real people rather than a generic ‘user’. I put myself in each persona’s shoes and think about how they would use the app.

    Competitor Analysis

    Competitor analysis is another tool I use before I start on wireframes or design anything. I did this competitive analysis for a South African online platform to promote and support a movement of active citizens to fix local challenges while holding authorities to account.

  2. 02

    Ideation

    Ideation is the stage where I can generate a wide range of possible solutions and explore different ways to solve the defined problem. High-fidelity wireframes may also allow for the creation of scaled-down or low-cost versions of the solution, to test and gather feedback before spending valuable time on the final design.

    Examples:

    Low-fidelity wireframes

    Low-fidelity wireframes show a rough idea of the placement of elements on a page, to enable quick decision-making without spending hours on page design and redesign.

    These were for a Greenfields project for a firm based in the UK. The organisation is for accountants who work in the public sector, accounting firms and other professional bodies where management of public funds is required, gathering statistics through customised questionnaires.

    High-fidelity wireframes

    High-fidelity wireframes give a more accurate idea of what the actual app design will look like, but without colour or pictures, and sometimes without the final icons. The final function of the app should be agreed upon using wireframes before moving on to the full visual design.

    I collaborated with a South African University to create high-fidelity wireframes for a surgery monitoring application using Figma. After three rounds of iterations, the final wireframes were approved. They only needed wireframes.

  3. 03

    Design

    Design is where the solution takes its final shape. For me, a screen that looks good isn’t enough: it also has to be easy to use. So I aim for clarity first, with clear navigation, key actions that are easy to find, and nothing on the screen that doesn’t need to be there. Then I sweat the details, such as colour, spacing, typography and states, and build them into a design system, so the result is consistent and developers can actually use it.

    Examples:

    Design tokens

    Tokens make it easier to hand a design over to developers.

    Building and maintaining design systems using variables is second nature to me. I routinely set up tokenised foundations for colour, spacing, and typography to create scalable, consistent, and developer-friendly design ecosystems.

    Design system

    Design systems make it easier to design a consistent application, with clear rules and reusable components.

    BBD's accelerator product Vanguard brings workflow, case management and orchestration capabilities through specific design patterns and modern tech approaches. We utilised Vanguard to update Metfriendly's (UK Metropolitan Police Friendly Society Limited) systems, and I created detailed screens in alignment with their brand guidelines, but before I could do that, using Figma, I had to create a design system for us to work with.

  4. 04

    Testing

    Good design shouldn’t rely on assumptions. I use testing as a continuous feedback loop—combining quick validation methods with deeper analytical insights—to understand real user behaviour and stress-test my decisions. This allows me to design with confidence, reduce friction, and create experiences that truly perform.

    Lyssna and user testing

    Lyssna is a user and market research platform for usability testing, interviews and surveys with a large, proprietary participant panel.

    Eye tracking and focus

    To evaluate the visual hierarchy and ensure key elements draw user attention, one of my tools is using the Attention Insight plugin in Figma. This tool simulates eye-tracking and highlights areas of focus based on contrast, layout, and design cues. It helps me validate that users would naturally gravitate toward call-to-actions and important content, allowing me to refine spacing, colours, and emphasis for better usability.

    AI User Testing

    I can also use AI-powered user testing tools to simulate how users might navigate through the interface, helping me identify potential friction points in the user flow. By analysing predicted interactions and behaviour patterns, I am able to spot areas where users might hesitate, get confused, or drop off. This early feedback allows me to iterate quickly and improve the overall experience before conducting live user tests.
