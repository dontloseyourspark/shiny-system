Design Process

My Iterative Design Process

The iterative design process is a dynamic approach to creating and refining digital experiences (in line with Agile methodologies). It involves cyclically gathering feedback, refining designs, and testing prototypes to continuously improve the user experience. By embracing iteration, designers can uncover insights, address user needs, and refine solutions over time, ultimately delivering more effective and user-friendly products.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    User Interviews

    I conducted user interviews to inform the design of a new note-taking application. I prepared an interview script with open-ended questions, focusing on my target audiences’ current note-taking process, pain points and frustrations with that process and features they would like in a new app. I recruited and interviewed some users remotely and some in person and then analysed my findings.

    Personas

    These personas were developed for a UK-based organisation that caters to accountants in the public sector, accounting firms, and professional bodies responsible for managing public funds, utilising custom questionnaires for statistical data collection. We wanted to form a deeper understanding of our users' goals, needs, experiences, and behaviours. So, we created 2 personas, one for each of our user segments.

    Competitor Analysis

    According to UX Design CC, a competitor analysis can be defined as

    “Identifying your competitors and evaluating their strategies to determine their strengths and weaknesses relative to those of your own product or service.”

    I did this competitive analysis for a South African online platform to promote and support a movement of active citizens to fix local challenges while holding authorities to account.

  2. 02

    Ideation

    Wireframes

    I collaborated with a South African University to create low-fidelity wireframes for a surgery monitoring application using Figma. After three rounds of iterations, the final wireframes were approved.

    This was for a Greenfields project for a firm based in the UK. The organisation is for accountants who work in the public sector, accounting firms and other professional bodies where management of public funds are required. Statistics gathering through customised questionnaires.

  3. 03

    Design

    Design System

    BBD's accelerator product Vanguard brings workflow, case management and orchestration capabilities through specific design patterns and modern tech approaches. We utilised Vanguard to update Metfriendly's (UK Metropolitan Police Friendly Society Limited) systems, and I created detailed screens in alignment with their brand guidelines, but before I could do that, using Figma, I had to create a design system for us to work with.

    Design Systems using variables/tokens

    Building and maintaining design systems using variables is second nature to me. I routinely set up tokenised foundations for colour, spacing, and typography to create scalable, consistent, and developer-friendly design ecosystems.

  4. 04

    Testing

    Testing

    Good design shouldn’t rely on assumptions. I use testing as a continuous feedback loop—combining quick validation methods with deeper analytical insights—to understand real user behaviour and stress-test my decisions. This allows me to design with confidence, reduce friction, and create experiences that truly perform.

    Lyssna and user testing

    Lyssna is a user and market research platform for usability testing, interviews and surveys with a large, proprietary participant panel.

    Eye tracking and focus

    To evaluate the visual hierarchy and ensure key elements draw user attention, one of my tools is using the Attention Insight plugin in Figma. This tool simulates eye-tracking and highlights areas of focus based on contrast, layout, and design cues. It helps me validate that users would naturally gravitate toward call-to-actions and important content, allowing me to refine spacing, colours, and emphasis for better usability.

    AI User Testing

    I can also use AI-powered user testing tools to simulate how users might navigate through the interface, helping me identify potential friction points in the user flow. By analysing predicted interactions and behaviour patterns, I am able to spot areas where users might hesitate, get confused, or drop off. This early feedback allows me to iterate quickly and improve the overall experience before conducting live user tests.
