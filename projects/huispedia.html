Huispedia

Dutch Real Estate platform

Overview

Huispedia is a fast-growing Dutch real estate platform, and I tackle the challenge of making complex housing journeys feel intuitive and actionable. By combining user insights with iterative design and testing, I deliver solutions that improve both user experience and business outcomes.

I’m a Senior Product Designer at Huispedia, where I’ve worked since December 2025 alongside developers, data scientists and the CTO.

I work with the data scientists to turn the output of their data and algorithms into something users can read at a glance. Examples are the Huispedia woningwaarde (home value), the “Ruim geschatte waarde” (estimated value range) and the bidding advice.

Key decisions

Making the bidding advice obvious

The problem

The banner that tells house hunters whether to expect overbidding was too vague and grey.

What made it hard

There are many things on this screen, so the hard part was the information architecture: choosing which part of the screen needs to grab your attention first. Users generally come to Huispedia to see how much they should bid on a house, so I wanted to make the overbidding or underbidding situation obvious.

What I chose, and why

I treated it as a hierarchy problem first: the bidding advice is what people come for, so it had to be the first thing the eye lands on. From there, their eye can travel through the rest of the screen at their leisure.

To make that happen, the banner became green so it stands out from the rest of the page, and the advice is colour-coded: orange for overbidding and light green for underbidding. Users can tell at a glance whether they should overbid, underbid or pay exactly what the house is worth.

Clicking the banner opens the Huispedia Plus modal, so the advice also leads people towards a subscription.

The result

I A/B tested the new banner, and users found the bidding advice much easier to find.

My Home: telling the story of your home’s value

I designed the new My Home screen together with another designer.

The problem

When homeowners view their own house on Huispedia, we wanted to show the important things they want to see: what their house is worth, how it compares to other houses in the neighbourhood, and how its value has changed compared to how the market is doing.

What made it hard

We didn’t want to show everything to people without a subscription. So how do you still offer value while encouraging people to subscribe to Huispedia Plus? Locking everything behind a paywall risked people leaving the page, and it would have hurt how the pages are found through search.

What we chose, and why

Together with the CTO, we decided to always show homeowners the value of their own home for free. To see insights into what is going on around them, they need a subscription.

The locked insights are a deliberate teaser: non-subscribers see a lock and blurred figures, so they can see what they’re missing.

We wanted to tell a story. It starts with the details of your house, which are always visible on the left, and shows the neighbourhood too, all above the fold. From there it can move on to what is happening further out.
